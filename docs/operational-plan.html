Going live — and what happens if something breaks.

The question your colleagues are asking is fair: can one person + AI cover production? The answer isn't "trust the AI" — it's a concrete operations plan that costs roughly one tenth of a dev team and addresses every realistic failure mode.

The choice in dollar terms

Recommended

Solo + AI + safeguards

$30k – $60k / year

All-in operating cost

  • Contractor on retainer (~$2-5k/mo for response coverage)
  • Vendor support tiers (Neon, Vercel, email)
  • Quarterly DR drill + dependency audit
  • Existing audit log + compliance posture
  • Same iteration speed as today
Alternative

Hire 2-person dev team

$300k+ / year

Salaries + benefits + recruiting

  • Two full-time engineers, fully loaded
  • 3-6 months ramp-up before they know the codebase
  • Process overhead (PRs, standups, planning)
  • Same compliance + vendor work still needed
  • Most time idle relative to actual incident volume

An honest read on AI in the loop

What AI handles well

  • Debugging. Reads stack traces, locates the bug, proposes a fix that compiles.
  • Codebase orientation. Every architectural decision is already documented inline in comments.
  • Boring bugs caught early. TypeScript + Prisma refuse to compile if types or schema drift.
  • Iteration speed. Features that would take a sprint with a dev land in an afternoon.
  • Documentation. Audit log, compliance page, changelog — already populated and current.

What AI does not handle

  • Being on-call at 2 a.m. A human still has to be the one in the loop when prod alerts fire.
  • Continuity if I step away. Sick, vacation, or departure — someone else needs to know the system.
  • Auditor + insurer conversations. SOC 2 attestations, vendor security questionnaires need a human signature.
  • Final architectural calls. AI advises and implements; "should we even do this" stays with a person.
  • Vendor relationships. Contract negotiations, incident escalations.

The operational plan, in six concrete pieces

1

Runbook in the repo

One RUNBOOK.md with rollback, DB restore, audit-log reading, vendor contacts. The on-call playbook.

½ day to write
2

Backup human on retainer

Contractor onboarded onto the codebase, available for emergencies. Insurance, not a teammate.

$2–5k / month
3

Documented escalation ladder

Time-boxed steps: try first, escalate at 30 min, surface to users at 2 hours. Predictable response posture.

Free · in repo
4

Quarterly disaster-recovery drill

Restore a DB snapshot to staging, verify boot, document time-to-restore. Proves the recovery story is real.

½ day / quarter
5

Vendor support contracts

Paid tiers with SLAs on Neon, Vercel, mail provider. Cheaper than a dev, addresses 80% of "what if infra fails."

$300–800 / month total
6

Quarterly upgrade + audit cadence

Dependency updates, security scan, code review of new paths. Documented. The thing auditors actually want.

1 day / quarter

If something breaks · the escalation ladder

T+0 Alert fires. Open Vercel dashboard, read recent deploy, check audit log. Most issues resolve here in 10 minutes — usually a deploy rollback.
T+30 min If unresolved, page the contractor. They have the runbook + codebase context already. Together we're a two-person team for the duration of the incident.
T+1 hour If vendor outage (DB / hosting / email), open a paid-tier support ticket. Most vendors have a 15-minute SLA on paid plans.
T+2 hours If user-facing, post a status banner via the editable-copy CMS already in place. Users see the situation, not a blank page.

The argument, in one sentence.

The question isn't "can AI replace programmers." The question is "what's the cheapest credible coverage plan that addresses each risk?"

A six-figure dev team and a five-figure operations plan both reach the same coverage. One spends ten times what the other does, and the codebase you'd hand over to that dev team — the documentation, the audit log, the compliance posture — already exists.

The one thing the operations plan does require: I have to be willing to be on call. That's the part no contract or AI removes — and it's the part the platform already needs from me regardless.
